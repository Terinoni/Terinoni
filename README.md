# Teresa Wanjiru Mbatia, PhD

### Human Geographer | Environmental & Climate Researcher | Spatial Intelligence | Urban & Environmental Governance

I am an interdisciplinary human geographer, researcher and practitioner working at the intersection of **climate, environment, cities, sustainability, governance and spatial justice**.

I am a Lecturer and Researcher at the **University of Nairobi** and Founder/Director of **Miji Bora Studios**. My research and professional work focuses on understanding how environmental and development challenges are shaped by spatial systems, institutions, communities and governance processes.

My work is particularly grounded in African urban and environmental contexts, with a strong interest in connecting **research evidence with practical decision-making**.

## Selected research

### 🌿 Urban Green Spaces & Environmental Justice

My doctoral research examined the political, social and spatial dimensions of urban green spaces and conservation in Nairobi.

The research explored:

- environmental justice
- spatial inequality
- conservation governance
- participation
- contested urban nature
- access to public environmental resources
- relationships between urbanisation and environmental change

**PhD:** Université Bordeaux Montaigne, France.

---

### 🌍 Urban National Parks & Conservation Governance

Research examining the relationship between protected nature and rapidly transforming cities, including questions of:

- urban conservation
- governance
- land-use change
- stakeholder interests
- socio-ecological systems
- urban development pressures

---

### 🍃 Karura Forest & Urban Eco-tourism

Research on the environmental, social and governance dimensions of urban nature and eco-tourism in Nairobi's Karura Forest.

**Selected publication:**

## Africa-centred environmental research

A major part of my work is grounded in **African cities, landscapes and institutions**.

I am interested in producing research that does not treat African contexts simply as sites where externally developed models or policies are applied, but instead recognises African cities, communities, ecosystems and institutions as important sources of knowledge for understanding environmental and climate transitions.

## Research philosophy

> **Good environmental decisions require more than good environmental data. They require understanding the places, institutions, people and power relations through which environmental change actually happens.**

My work therefore seeks to connect **evidence, spatial realities, environmental systems and governance** to support more grounded approaches to sustainability and climate action.
